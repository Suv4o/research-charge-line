# Weather for Premium: sources, cost, and where it goes (2026-11-03)

The user asked for weather in the app for Premium users, as cheaply as possible: "it will show the weather conditions on the arrival destination or something like that". They also asked for the best way to add it, and where in the app it should go.

## Summary

- **Weather matters more to the plan than to the screen.**
  - The app's energy model ([EnergyModel.swift](../../app/ChargeLine/Planner/EnergyModel.swift)) plans every trip at 22 °C, with no wind, on dry roads. It already has an outside-temperature input; nothing feeds it yet.
  - With the catalog Model Y at 110 km/h, compared with that calm 22 °C day (§1):
    - 0 °C uses about **12% more** energy.
    - A 20 km/h headwind at the car uses **30% more**: range drops from about 427 km to 328 km.
    - A wet road adds about **6%**.
  - So the best Premium feature is twofold: **show the weather at each stop and on arrival, and plan with the forecast**. ABRP's equivalent is also Premium-only ([ABRP help](https://abrp.featurebase.app/en/help/articles/6430985-real-time-weather)).
- **Apple's WeatherKit can't be behind Premium as a weather display.**
  - Apple's terms: "You may not charge any fees to end users for access to or use of the Apple Weather Data presented in its original form". Only "Value-Added Services or Products" that transform the data may be paid ([Apple Developer Program License Agreement](https://developer.apple.com/support/downloads/terms/apple-developer-program/Apple-Developer-Program-License-Agreement-English.pdf), Attachment 8 §1.2).
  - So "17°, light rain when you arrive" from WeatherKit would have to be free for everyone. Only the weather-adjusted plan could be Premium.
- **Recommended source: MET Norway, through Charge Line's server.**
  - The Norwegian Meteorological Institute's forecast API is free, CC BY 4.0, and commercial use is fine. For Australia it uses ECMWF's 9 km global model.
  - Its terms want apps to go through a caching proxy. Charge Line's server can be that proxy, sharing each answer between all drivers on the same highway.
  - Cost: **A$0 at 2,000, 10,000 and 50,000 users**. The server's added traffic is about 1 GB a month at 50,000 users (§3).
  - Tested 2026-11-03 on Goulburn: 39 KB per place; hourly for about 2.5 days, then 6-hourly to 9 days; cacheable for about 30 minutes.
- **Alternatives, for comparison** (Premium only, 5% of users):
  - **Google Weather API:** about US$1 a month at 2,000 users, US$13 at 10,000 and US$59 at 50,000. Paid display is allowed.
  - **Open-Meteo:** a flat US$29 a month for commercial use.
  - **WeatherKit:** free to about 19,000 users, then US$99.99 a month at 50,000, with the display free for everyone.
  - **Bureau of Meteorology:** its free data is "not for commercial use". Paid feeds start at about A$3,000 in the first year (A$1,335 set-up, A$1,282 registration and a A$358 bundle).
- **Where in the app:**
  - a "Weather on the way" card on the trip screen;
  - a small weather mark beside each stop's and the destination's arrival time;
  - "While you charge" weather on a stop's details;
  - a Premium badge row for free users (§4).
- **Effort:** about 4–6 days in total.
  - Server endpoint about 1 day.
  - App client and route sampling about 1 day.
  - Planner changes 1–2 days.
  - Screens, credits and texts 1–2 days.
  - Weather warnings along the route are a later, separate step (§5).

## 1. What weather does to an EV's energy use

**How the model works.** The planner cuts the route into 1 km pieces. Each piece's energy is road load `A + B·v + C·v²` over the distance, plus climbing, plus cabin and electronics power by temperature (`accessoryKW(ambientC:)`). All of it is scaled by the car's learned correction ([RouteProfile.swift](../../app/ChargeLine/Planner/RouteProfile.swift)). Today `ambientC` is always 22, with no wind and no rain.

**Calculation:** the same equations, for the catalog's 2025 Model Y Long Range AWD (EPA A = 37.77 lbf, B = −0.1538, C = 0.02044; 75 kWh usable), at a steady 110 km/h:

| Conditions | Wh/km | vs 22 °C, calm, dry | Range on 75 kWh |
|---|---|---|---|
| 22 °C, calm, dry (today's plan) | 175.5 | | 427 km |
| 5 °C | 191.0 | +8.8% | 393 km |
| 0 °C | 196.8 | +12.1% | 381 km |
| 35 °C | 177.6 | +1.2% | 422 km |
| 40 °C | 179.2 | +2.1% | 419 km |
| Headwind 10 km/h | 201.0 | +14.5% | 373 km |
| Headwind 20 km/h | 228.7 | +30.3% | 328 km |
| Headwind 30 km/h | 258.6 | +47.3% | 290 km |
| Tailwind 20 km/h | 131.2 | −25.3% | 572 km |
| Wet road (rolling resistance +30%) | 187.0 | +6.5% | 401 km |
| 5 °C + 20 km/h headwind + wet | 258.9 | +47.5% | 290 km |

**How each effect is modelled**
- **Temperature** changes two things.
  - Cabin heating or cooling, from the app's table, anchored to fueleconomy.gov and Liu et al. 2018.
  - Air density, ρ ∝ 1/T, which scales the drag term. For example, 5 °C air is 6% denser than 22 °C air.
  - AAA's dynamometer tests found range fell 41% at −7 °C with the heater on and 17% at 35 °C with the air conditioning on, in mixed driving ([AAA via news syndication](https://abcnews4.com/news/auto-matters/aaa-cold-weather-can-cut-electric-car-range-over-40-percent)). Australia rarely gets that cold, but inland winter mornings of 0–5 °C are common.
- **Wind:** drag is `C·(v + headwind)²`. At 110 km/h, drag is 78% of this car's road force, which is why wind matters so much.
  - Forecasts give wind at 10 m. Near the ground it is slower: on open country, about 0.6–0.7 of the 10 m figure, by the standard log wind profile.
  - So a forecast 30 km/h headwind acts like about 20 km/h at the car. The planner should use the headwind component along each 1 km piece's heading, scaled that way.
- **Rain:** a Chalmers thesis measured about 6% more road resistance at 30 km/h and 11% at 120 km/h on very wet roads ([Chalmers ODR](https://odr.chalmers.se/items/aabb0e8b-e459-4971-bf3f-b896ef959537)). That's a thesis, not peer-reviewed. The model's "+30% rolling resistance" gives about +6.5% overall, at the low end of that range.

**What this means for a trip.** A windy, cold day can need a whole extra charging stop on a 400 km leg, which the plan doesn't know about today. The trip screen's footnote already says plans don't count cold. The preferences already say the arrival buffer is "room for detours, headwinds and a busy charger".

- **Google:** each successful request is billed. Hourly forecasts come 24 hours to a page, and each page is a request.

## 3. Cost at 2,000, 10,000 and 50,000 users

**Assumptions** (as in the 2026-10-29 and 2026-11-02 notes):
- 5% of users are Premium.
- Premium users plan 8 charging trips a month; free users 4.

**Weather per trip**
- About 8 places: one every ~75 km along the route, each charging stop, and the destination.
- Each is fetched twice: when planned, and when the trip is opened again later.
- So **128 place lookups per Premium user a month**.
- Google needs about 1.5 pages per place, for trips that leave tomorrow.
- WeatherKit's display has to be free, so free users would fetch the destination and stops too: about 20 lookups a month each.

| Source and setup | 2,000 users | 10,000 users | 50,000 users |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MET Norway through our server** (Premium display and planning) | **$0** | **$0** | **$0** (about 1 GB a month more server traffic) |
| Google Weather, Premium only | ~US$1.40 | ~US$13 | ~US$59 |
| Open-Meteo Standard, Premium only | US$29 | US$29 | US$29 |
| OpenWeather One Call, Premium only | £0 | ~£41 | ~£350 |
| WeatherKit: display free for everyone, planning for Premium | $0 (51k calls) | $0 (254k calls) | US$99.99 (1.27M calls: the 2M tier) |
| WeatherKit: planning only, no weather shown | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| Bureau of Meteorology: hourly forecasts for set places plus registration | ~A$3,700 in the first year, ~A$2,400 a year after | same | same |

For scale, Premium brings in about US$300 a month at 2,000 users, US$1,500 at 10,000 and US$7,500 at 50,000 (2026-10-29 note).

**MET's own limit isn't close.** At 50,000 users there would be about 320,000 lookups a month, about 0.1 a second on average. The server's shared cache (one entry per ~10 km square, kept until MET's `Expires`) answers many of them, against MET's limit of 20 a second.

## 4. The design

### Where it goes

1. **Trip screen: a "Weather on the way" card**, under the Total / Charging / Stops / Arrive tiles:
   ```
   ┌ Weather on the way ───────────────────────────────┐
   │ Sydney when you arrive, 4:35 pm   ☁︎ 17°  light rain │
   │ 9–17° on the way · headwind up to 25 km/h after   │
   │ Goulburn · showers from 3 pm                      │
   │ Planned with the forecast: about 8% more energy    │
   │ than a calm, mild day.                            │
   │ Forecast from MET Norway, 2:10 pm                 │
   └───────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
   ```
   - This answers "the weather on arrival" and explains why a plan has an extra stop.
   - Free users see one row instead, "Weather on the way" with the Premium badge. It opens the paywall, like the "Chargers along the route" button.
2. **Timeline:** a small SF Symbol and temperature next to each stop's and the destination's arrival time ("1:10 pm · 🌧 14°"). Premium only.
3. **A stop's details:** "While you charge (1:10–1:35 pm): 14°, rain, wind 20 km/h". The driver will be standing outside.
4. **Footnote and credits**
   - The estimate line becomes "…the route's speeds, hills and the weather forecast".
   - Add "Weather from MET Norway (CC BY 4.0)", in the card and in Legal.
5. **Paywall:** a new reason `.weather`: "Weather on the way comes with Premium." / "See the weather at each stop and when you arrive, with plans that count headwinds, cold and rain."
6. **A setting:** "Plan with the weather forecast", on by default for Premium. Drivers who want the calm-day estimate can turn it off. ABRP has the same switch (Settings → Routing → Weather).
7. **Later:** CarPlay's trip view could show the next stop's weather. CarPlay is already Premium-only.

### How it works

**Server (`server/`):** `POST /v1/weather` with up to about 24 points (lat, lng) and a time window.
- The server rounds each point to 0.1°, so drivers share answers.
- It asks MET's `compact` forecast, with the same public User-Agent as Overpass, `If-Modified-Since` and a few requests at a time.
- It keeps each answer in memory until its `Expires`.
- It returns only the hours asked for, per point: time, temperature, wind speed and direction, rain in mm, and the symbol code.
- It needs the app key and gets its own daily limit bucket, like the other routes.
- Because the app talks only to our server, MET can be swapped for Google or Open-Meteo later without an app update.

**App:** a weather client for that endpoint.
- During planning, for Premium only, it fetches weather alongside the hills (`TripPlanningService.profile`): for points about every 75 km along each route option, plus the destination.
- It gets each 1 km piece's weather from the nearest point, at the time the car is there (departure plus driving time).
- Changing the departure time re-reads the same forecast with no new lookup. MET's hourly data runs about 60 hours ahead; after that it's 6-hourly, and there's none beyond about 9 days.
- A trip further off says "The forecast appears 9 days before you leave" and plans for a calm, mild day as now.

**Planner (`EnergyModel` / `RouteProfile`):** each piece gets optional weather.
- Temperature feeds cabin power and air density.
- The headwind component, at about 0.65 of the 10 m wind, feeds the drag term.
- Rain over about 0.5 mm an hour raises rolling resistance by 30%.
- Without weather, everything stays as it is today, so free plans and the tests don't change.
- Keep the arrival buffer: a forecast can be wrong.
- **Learning from trips:** the learned correction `k_car` should compare the drive with the weather-adjusted prediction. Otherwise one windy trip skews it.

**Texts**
- Privacy policy: "points along your route go to Charge Line's server, which asks MET Norway for the forecast". MET sees only the server, never the phone.
- Terms: forecasts can be wrong.
- Listing: add it to the Premium features and the review notes.
- No App Privacy label change: precise location already goes to our server, for app functionality, not linked.

## 5. Later: weather warnings on the route

Warnings would be worth showing on Australian road trips: severe thunderstorms, floods, bushfire weather. Three ways to get them:
- **Google Weather API `publicAlerts`:** Australia is covered. Each alert has its polygon, severity and publisher ([reference](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/weather/reference/rest/v1/publicAlerts/lookup)).
  - For Premium, about 6 lookups per trip fetch: about $0 at 2,000 users, US$6 a month at 10,000 and US$30 at 50,000.
  - Which Australian agencies feed it is unconfirmed; one test call would show it. Google's Public Alerts have carried NSW RFS and SA CFS warnings ([PreventionWeb](https://www.preventionweb.net/news/australia-online-warnings-when-threat-high)).
- **BoM warnings bundle with CAP:** A$358 a year, plus the A$1,282 registration and A$1,335 set-up. The official source.
- **WeatherKit:** not an option. No Australian alert source is listed, and its terms forbid changing alert text.

## 6. Open questions

- Which agencies' warnings Google's Weather API returns in Australia. One test call answers it.
- How well a 9 km model's wind predicts wind at car height on Australian highways. After launch, compare planned and recorded trips with and without the weather adjustment, using the learning data.
- Whether free users should get anything: for example, only the arrival temperature. Not needed for 1.0.
